# Glossary

Plain-English definitions of terms used throughout this coaching system.
Every agent should link back to or reuse these definitions instead of
inventing new wording. Add a term here the first time it's needed.

## Business model

- **Dropshipping** — Selling products online without holding any
  inventory. A customer orders from your store, you then buy that same
  item from a supplier at a lower price, and the supplier ships it
  directly to the customer. Your profit is the difference.
- **Niche** — A specific category or theme your store focuses on (e.g.
  "kitchen gadgets" or "dog accessories"), instead of selling everything.
- **Supplier** — The company that actually manufactures or stocks the
  product and ships it to your customer when you place an order.
- **Fulfillment** — The process of getting a paid order actually shipped
  to the customer (in dropshipping, this means placing the matching order
  with your supplier).
- **SKU** (Stock Keeping Unit) — A unique code identifying one specific
  product/variant, used to track inventory and orders.
- **Saturated (product/market)** — So many other sellers are already
  selling this exact product that it's hard to stand out or profit from
  it.
- **Winning product** — A product that's proven to sell well and
  profitably, usually validated with real ad/sales data, not just a guess.

## Business & legal (US / New York specific)

- **Sole proprietorship** — The default, no-paperwork business structure
  when one person runs a business alone. Simple, but you're personally
  liable for business debts.
- **LLC** (Limited Liability Company) — A registered business structure
  that separates your personal assets from business debts/lawsuits.
  Costs money and paperwork to set up and maintain in NY.
- **EIN** (Employer Identification Number) — A free federal tax ID
  number for a business, issued by the IRS, used like a Social Security
  Number for the business. Get it at irs.gov — never pay a third party
  for this.
- **Sales tax** — A percentage tax added to sales of physical goods,
  collected from the customer and sent to the state. New York requires a
  **Certificate of Authority** before you can legally collect it.
- **Certificate of Authority** — The permit NY State issues that legally
  allows a business to collect sales tax. Apply free at tax.ny.gov.
- **Nexus** — The legal connection that requires you to collect sales tax
  in a state (e.g. having a business location there, or selling enough
  volume there).
- **Bookkeeping** — Recording every dollar in and out of the business so
  you know your real financial position and can file taxes correctly.
- **1099-K** — A tax form payment processors (like Shopify Payments/PayPal)
  send you and the IRS reporting how much you were paid through them in a
  year.

## Store & platform

- **Shopify** — The ecommerce platform used to build and run the online
  store.
- **Theme** — The visual template/design for a Shopify store.
- **Product page** — The page on the store where a single product is
  shown and sold.
- **Policy pages** — Required/expected legal pages on a store: shipping
  policy, return/refund policy, privacy policy, and terms of service.
- **Checkout** — The process a customer goes through to pay and complete
  an order.
- **Shopify Payments** — Shopify's built-in way to accept credit/debit
  card payments, with fees automatically deducted.
- **Domain** — The web address of the store (e.g. `mystore.com`), bought
  separately from Shopify or through Shopify.
- **Password protection** — A Shopify setting that hides the store behind
  a password so the public can't see it until you're ready to launch.

## Content & marketing

- **Organic (traffic/testing)** — Views or visitors that come for free
  (like people finding your TikTok video), as opposed to paid ads.
- **Hook** — The first 1-3 seconds of a video, designed to stop someone
  from scrolling past.
- **Shot list** — A written plan of the specific video clips needed
  before filming, so nothing gets missed.
- **UGC** (User-Generated Content) — Video/photo content that looks
  like it was made by a normal customer rather than a polished ad,
  often used because it performs better and feels more trustworthy.
- **CTA** (Call to Action) — The instruction telling the viewer what to
  do next (e.g. "tap the link to shop").

## Advertising

- **TikTok Ads / Meta Ads** — Paid advertising platforms (Meta runs
  Facebook and Instagram ads) that show your content to more people than
  would see it for free, in exchange for money.
- **Ad spend** — Money paid directly to an ad platform to run ads.
- **Impressions** — The number of times an ad was shown.
- **CTR** (Click-Through Rate) — The percentage of people who saw the ad
  and clicked it. Formula: clicks ÷ impressions × 100.
- **CPC** (Cost Per Click) — How much, on average, each click on the ad
  cost. Formula: ad spend ÷ clicks.
- **CPA** (Cost Per Acquisition, aka cost per sale) — How much ad spend
  it took, on average, to get one sale. Formula: ad spend ÷ number of
  sales.
- **ROAS** (Return On Ad Spend) — Revenue earned per dollar of ad spend.
  Formula: revenue ÷ ad spend. A ROAS of 2.0 means $2 back for every $1
  spent (before other costs).
- **Conversion** — When a visitor completes the desired action, usually
  making a purchase.
- **Pixel** — A small tracking snippet installed on the store that tells
  the ad platform when someone who saw an ad took an action (like buying),
  so the platform can optimize and you can measure results.
- **A/B test** — Running two versions of something (an ad, a headline) at
  the same time to see which performs better.
- **Kill/keep rule** — A pre-decided, unemotional rule for when to stop
  (kill) or continue (keep) spending on an ad or product, based on numbers
  instead of gut feeling.

## Money & numbers

- **Retail price** — The price the customer pays.
- **Unit cost** — What you pay the supplier for one item.
- **Margin** — Profit as a percentage of revenue. Formula: net profit ÷
  retail price × 100.
- **Net profit (per sale)** — What's actually left after every cost:
  retail price − unit cost − shipping cost − payment processing fee − ad
  cost.
- **Payment processing fee** — The cut a payment processor takes for
  handling a card payment (typically a percentage plus a flat fee per
  transaction).
- **Break-even** — The point where revenue exactly equals costs — no
  profit, no loss.
- **Chargeback** — When a customer disputes a charge with their bank/card
  company and the money is forcibly returned, often with an extra fee to
  the merchant.
- **Ledger** — A simple running record of every transaction (expenses and
  sales), used to track real profit over time.
